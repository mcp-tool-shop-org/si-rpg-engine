Depends on T2 and T4, both merged; runs beside F1, which changes the law, so T6 changes nothing the law is made of (pin 12). The plan row is T6 in `docs/PHASE-2.md`. ## What it is

Go-Explore guided by a navmesh kept an archive of cells, returned to a chosen cell by restoring its state, and explored from there; on a 1.5 km world it found fall-through-floor bugs with no human demonstrations (Lu, Georgescu, and Verwey 2022, arXiv:2209.00570). Walkable space rebuilt from the geometry and checked against the navmesh's own answers finds the places where the two disagree (Raghavan et al. 2026, arXiv:2605.21397). And the agents that test a game must drive the build that ships, not a test build of it (Gillberg et al. 2023, arXiv:2307.11105).

This engine has the pieces. A world restores from an image of the physics module's memory (T2). Every action goes through a checker that admits or refuses it (the verbs). The character's limits are measured (T4). T6 explores a world's reachable states with the admitted verbs, through the pinned binary, and compares where bodies can get with the zones the file authored. A world with an authored zone that nothing reaches is refused with the zone named. So is a world from which an admitted action carries a body out of the world. Every claim the sweep makes comes with a witness: a log of intents from the load state that `replay` reproduces.

## Pins

1. **A tick restores without replay.** Every restore today replays to the save tick and then puts the world half back (`world.save` and `world.restore` in `packages/tick/world.js`); the tick's own state is rebuilt by replay. The sweep branches from saved states thousands of times, so the tick gains `save()` and `restore(saved)` that pair `world.save()` with the tick's own state: the tick number, the running hasher's lanes, the current frame, the scheduled actions, the quanta owed to non-action admissions, the minds' memory, and the input log itself. The log is copied, not cut back to a length, because the sweep restores states that different branches reached, and a restored tick's log must be the path to that state. A save taken while an action is in flight restores it too; the sweep saves only settled states, but a host saving a game will not. Restoring and then advancing traces identically to the uninterrupted run from that tick. The proof uses T2's harness, extended: every fixture and the product scene, at T2's chosen points, restored into a tick that has since run elsewhere, twice in a row, with no replay before the restore. A planted omission of one saved field fails that test; the test shows it for at least the hasher, a scheduled action, and a mind's memory.

2. **Restore as an inner loop.** A memory restore costs about 56 ms in CI today (T5's corpus log). That includes a byte-by-byte digest of the whole 32 MiB image and a fresh instance of the module, and the builder measures which dominates. The sweep keeps each archived state's image in the process, sparse, as T5 stores it: a bitmap of the 512 pages and only the pages that are not all zero. It restores such an image without leaving the process and with the same checks in kind: the same binary, a whole number of pages, and the image's digest. Two things may make that cheap, and the builder measures before choosing. The digest of a page of zeros is a fixed multiplication of each lane's state, so the whole-image digest can be computed from the pages in use. And a running instance whose stack pointer is at its base may take the image in place, since its memory then is its whole state, while an instance that has trapped still gets a fresh one. This touches only the generated glue in `solver/build.mjs`, as its own commit. It does not change the Rust source, the law, or the binary's bytes, and the dense form stays what a bundle carries across processes. Pin 1's harness proves the in-process restore traces identically, and a test shows it refuses an image from another binary and one with a changed byte.

3. **Cells.** The archive maps a cell to the first state that reached it: a saved tick (pins 1 and 2), the witness, meaning the log of admitted intents from the load state that reached it, and the quanta that path took. A cell is: the actor's id; the actor's centre on a grid of pitch p on all three axes; the id of the body it carries, or `-`; and the zone index of every other dynamic body. The pitch p is the actor's smaller horizontal full extent (0.5 for the walker of `worlds/crate-and-door.json`). A state is archived only when it is settled: no action scheduled, and every body asleep or at rest within 512 quanta of the action ending, the load hazard's own criterion. The module documents the cell key, and a change to it is named in the pull request that makes it.

4. **Actors.** The actors are the goal's actor, every body with a mind, and, for a fixture, every body its log drives. The sweep explores one actor at a time while the other bodies take no action, and a zone counts as reached when any sweep reaches it. A world with no actor has nothing to sweep, and `load world` says so.

5. **Actions, only through the checker.** From each archived cell the sweep restores its state and submits, one at a time, each admitted verb aimed at these targets. A point target is a ground-plane point `{ x, z }`, as every point target is. Move aims at the centres of the eight neighbouring grid cells. Climb, and drop while carrying, aim one, two, and three cells out in the same eight directions, within the rule's `maxDistance`, because a ledge's edge or a place to set a body down need not fall on the cell next to the actor; a sweep that aimed only one cell out would call a climbable ledge unreachable. The builder may change these reaches with measurements, and names the change. A body-target verb (push, pick-up) aims at each dynamic body within the rule's `maxDistance`. The episode verb (`use`) changes nothing in the world and is not swept. Each proposal cites the newest frame's hash and goes through `submit`. A refused proposal is counted as refused and costs nothing more; an admitted one runs to completion and then to settle. The sweep never writes a body, never calls the solver directly, and never proposes a retired verb. This is the whole action set. A route that needs something outside it, such as two actors at once or a target off the grid, is not explored, and the sweep's report names the action set, so a refusal reads as "not reached by these actions", never as a proof of the impossible.

6. **Order and determinism.** The frontier is breadth-first in the order cells were archived, and actions are tried in an order the module states. Sweeping a world twice gives the same archive, the same witnesses, and the same verdicts, and a test proves it.

7. **Verdicts and findings.** For each authored zone the sweep reports whether some explored state held some body's centre in it, with the first witness that did. It reports three findings, each written as a T5 bundle (a `log` bundle whose log is the witness plus the action) that `replay` reproduces: an admitted action after which a body is below the lowest collider's minimum; an admitted action after which the tick throws; and an admitted action after which the world does not settle within 512 quanta.

8. **Witnesses are proofs.** Every witness replays from the load state through the ordinary replay path: submitting its intents at their ticks reproduces the sweep's own hashes along the path exactly. A test replays every zone's witness for every world it sweeps and compares. A planted restore that omits one saved field fails this test. It is the check that exploring by restore explores the real world and not a copy that drifted from it.

9. **The load hazard.** `load world` runs the sweep after the settle hazard, under a budget of quanta and restores that the code states with its measured cost. When the frontier empties within the budget, three things are refused. An authored zone that no explored state reached is refused, naming the zone, the actors, the cells explored, and the action set. A body leaving the world is refused, and so is a throw, each with its bundle's path. A world that does not settle after an action is reported with its bundle and admitted: whether that refuses is decided after the scheduled job shows how often it fires, because a character on a slope near the controller's limit creeps (T4) and refusing it now would refuse sound content. When the budget runs out first, the world is admitted, and `load world` prints that the sweep was deferred, with the cells archived and the frontier left. The scheduled job then sweeps it (pin 10). The index format does not change.

10. **The scheduled sweep.** The weekly corpus job (`.github/workflows/corpus.yml`) sweeps every world in `worlds/index.json`, every fixture world that runs on the product law, and the product scene, with a larger budget it states. A refusal or a finding is a failure, handled by T5's machinery: the bundles are uploaded and one issue is opened. The job still never pushes. It stays inside its timeout, the pull request states the job's new wall time, and the standards compliance at the top of the workflow file is updated.

11. **Tests.**
    - A world whose zone is walled in on four sides by colliders taller than the climb's `maxRise` is refused with that zone named. The same world with one wall removed is admitted, with a witness into the zone, so the refusal is not a sweep that reaches nothing.
    - A world whose zone sits on a plateau higher than `maxRise` above every approach is refused with the zone named. The same plateau at a rise the climb covers is admitted with a witness that climbs.
    - A world with a gap in its floor that the walker can step into is refused with a body-leaves-the-world finding, and `replay` on its bundle reproduces the fall.
    - `worlds/crate-and-door.json` is admitted. Its door zone's witness replays, and the test prints its cells, actions, quanta, restores, and wall time.
    - Pin 1's restore tests with the planted omissions, pin 2's refusals, pin 6's determinism test, and pin 8's witness replays with the planted omission.
    - Every world and product-law fixture world that loads today still loads. If the sweep refuses one, the pull request names it and why: a refusal of existing content is a finding for the coordinator, not a test to relax.

12. **Nothing the law is made of.** T6 changes no Rust source, no law, and no byte of the binary; the goldens and the Linux digest do not move. F1 is changing the law in parallel. If F1 merges first, T6 rebases onto it, and any number T6 pins that F1 moves is measured again, never loosened.

13. **Costs on record.** The pull request states, for `worlds/crate-and-door.json` and for the product scene in the scheduled job: cells archived, actions tried and admitted, quanta run, restores, wall time, and the share of wall time spent restoring, before and after pin 2.

14. **Docs are the lead's.** The builder does not edit `README.md`, `README.*.md`, `site/`, or `CHANGELOG.md`; the coordinator writes the public surfaces after merge.

15. **The map** is regenerated on Linux with the published `@dogfood-lab/atlas@1.17.0` after the new files are committed, because the map covers tracked files only.

## Acceptance

- A world with an authored zone no body can reach is refused with the zone named, and the same world opened up is admitted with a witness.
- The tick restores without replay and traces identically on every fixture and the product scene, and each planted omission goes red.
- The in-process restore is measured before and after, refuses what the full restore refuses, and traces identically.
- Every reached zone's witness replays to the sweep's own hashes.
- A floor gap is refused with a bundle that reproduces the fall.
- `corpus.yml` sweeps the indexed worlds, the fixture worlds, and the product scene on one `workflow_dispatch` run of the branch, green, linked in the pull request.
- Typecheck clean; tests at or above the count on `main`; goldens and digest unchanged; Atlas check green; two push-triggered workflow files.

## Not in T6

No change to the law or the binary. No model in the loop; that is T7. No learned exploration policy: the action set is fixed and exhaustive over its grid, since the worlds this engine holds are small enough not to need one yet. No route through two actors at once. No refusal of a world that does not settle after an action until the scheduled job has measured how often it happens.
