# Study-swarm dispatch: what the solver has to be

> **Product being decided.** `si-rpg-engine` E2. The law is a fixed-timestep step hashed every quantum, three-dimensional since E1, on axis-aligned boxes and the five exactly-specified float operations, proven byte-identical under V8, SpiderMonkey, and JavaScriptCore. The question is what the step must become for a 3D RPG world, and whether that is a growth of the law in JavaScript or one WebAssembly binary from Rust.

## Step 1 — Load-bearing questions

- **QA Solver determinism.** Does any Rust or WASM rigid-body solver carry a determinism claim that covers three browser engines, and at what cost?
- **QB Character traversal.** What shapes and controller model do production engines use for characters on slopes, stairs, and props, and where do boxes fail?
- **QC Arithmetic.** Did shipped lockstep games stay on floats or move to fixed point, and what actually caused their desyncs?
- **QD Solver state and shapes.** What solver-internal state survives a step and must be hashed or forbidden, and which shapes beyond boxes a replayable world can have?

## Step 3 — Research grounding

### QA Solver determinism

1. **No shipped WASM physics build has its determinism proven across the three browser engines; Jolt's CI hashes five scenes across MSVC, Clang, GCC on x86, ARM, RISC-V, PowerPC, LoongArch and emscripten WASM32/64, but the WASM jobs run under Node, so only V8.** Rouwé, Jolt Physics `Architecture.md` and `determinism_check.yml`, opened 2026-09-24 (https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics). Implication: a library's own CI does not stand in for our golden; the three-engine run stays ours.
2. **Jolt's cross-platform mode costs about 8% and requires no FP contraction, precise float mode, round-to-nearest, consistent DAZ/FTZ, no std sin/cos/sort/hash, and the same source and defines on every platform.** Same source. Implication: the flag list is the contract any WASM build must carry, written into the build, not the README.
3. **Rapier v0.35 (2026) rests determinism on a shared scalar core with transcendentals pinned to libm, canonicalizes signed zeros in contact impulses because they had diverged across platforms, and states the feature "costs no measurable performance anymore"; its JS `rapier3d-deterministic` package is the no-SIMD build, and its docs claim cross-browser agreement with no test in CI behind the claim.** Dimforge, `CHANGELOG.md` v0.35.0 and `bindings/typescript/builds`, and https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/determinism, opened 2026-09-24. Implication: signed zero is a divergence source our hash must treat like NaN; a claim without a test is a claim.
4. **Rapier issue #868 (2025, closed): a 2×2×2 ball drop diverged between two runs on one machine without `enhanced-determinism`, against the documented local guarantee; a regression test now exists.** https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/issues/868. Implication: local determinism is not free even in a solver that advertises it; the golden is the only proof that survives a version bump.
5. **Box2D v3 reached cross-platform determinism with FMA contraction off, no fast-math, and a custom atan2f, verified by a step count and a transform hash across MSVC, Clang, GCC, x64, ARM, Apple M2 against Ryzen, with no noticeable performance impact; WASM is not mentioned.** Catto 2024 (https://box2d.org/posts/2024/08/determinism/). Implication: the Falling Hinges shape, a hash plus a count to sleep, is the shape of our product golden.
6. **Avian's determinism hash is a 2D port of Falling Hinges, its full OS matrix runs only on labelled PRs, and there is no WASM target.** https://docs.rs/avian3d and its `rust.yaml`, opened 2026-09-24. Implication: not a candidate for a 3D golden.
7. **WebAssembly fixed-width SIMD applies the scalar rules per lane and is deterministic; NaN sign and payload are nondeterministic when a non-canonical NaN enters; only relaxed SIMD is host-dependent.** W3C WebAssembly spec numerics and profiles; relaxed-simd Overview.md, opened 2026-09-24. Implication: a WASM build may use `simd128`, must not use relaxed SIMD, and NaN stays out of the hash.

### QB Character traversal

8. **PhysX's character controller is a kinematic collide-and-slide, chosen over dynamic bodies to avoid tunnelling, bouncing, unwanted rotation, and unrealistic friction; it offers a non-rotating box or a capsule and recommends the capsule for stairs; without auto-stepping a box character gets stuck on slight bumps; it pushes nothing automatically.** NVIDIA PhysX 5.4.1 Character Controllers (https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.1/docs/CharacterControllers.html). Implication: our walker, a dynamic box under gravity resolved by smallest-axis push-out, is the configuration PhysX documents as failing; the character must become a kinematic controller.
9. **Unity's CharacterController is a capsule that climbs stairs below Step Offset, walks slopes within Slope Limit, gets stuck when Skin Width is too small, ignores forces, and does not push rigidbodies.** Unity Manual (https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-CharacterController.html). Implication: step height, slope limit, and skin width are the controller's three parameters; pushing is a verb, as it already is here.
10. **Unreal's CharacterMovementComponent is a capsule with `max_step_height`, `walkable_floor_angle`, a perch threshold against the capsule's rounded edge, and a flat-base floor check because a capsule "slowly lowers off the side of a ledge."** Epic, UE 5.4 API (https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/CharacterMovementComponent?application_version=5.4). Implication: ledges need a flat-base floor test, which a box gives for free.
11. **Godot's CharacterBody3D has a floor angle, snap length, and slide count and no step property; its troubleshooting page says bodies catch on internal edges between adjacent tile colliders and the fix is one collider per island, and that boxes are the most reliable character shape while capsules ease diagonals and cylinders are unstable.** Godot docs (https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_characterbody3d.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/troubleshooting_physics_issues.html). Implication: static props merge per island at load; a box character is defensible if it has auto-step.
12. **Jolt's `CharacterVirtual` is not a body in the world, steps stairs and sticks to ground through `ExtendedUpdate`, and affects dynamic bodies only through impulses it applies; Rapier's controller recommends cuboid, ball, or capsule, keeps an offset gap, has slope climb and slide angles, autostep with a minimum step-top width, and snap-to-ground.** Jolt docs (https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html); Rapier character controller guide (https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/character_controller). Implication: the controller model is the same in every engine opened; the law should have one.
13. Removed at the Director's instruction, 2026-09-24. The finding carried market and presentation framing that has no place in this engine's record, and it contributed nothing to the decision. Numbering is kept so later references hold.

### QC Arithmetic

14. **IEEE 754 requires add, subtract, multiply, divide, and square root to be exactly rounded, so compliant machines agree in every bit on those; transcendentals are deliberately not required to be.** Goldberg 1991, ACM Computing Surveys (https://docs.oracle.com/cd/E19957-01/806-3568/ncg_goldberg.html). Implication: the five-operation contract is the standard's own reproducibility boundary.
15. **Results change between IEEE-compliant platforms through x87 double rounding, register spills, optimisation level, and the compiler's choice to fuse multiply-add; `sin` of one input differs in its last bits across CPUs and libcs.** Monniaux 2008, ACM TOPLAS 30(3) (arXiv:cs/0701192; https://hal.science/hal-00128124v5/document). Implication: a compiled build pins contraction and precision or it is a different law.
16. **Supreme Commander and Demigod stayed on floats, forced single precision and round-to-nearest at startup, asserted both every tick, and report zero float desyncs across over a million customers; their problems came from third-party physics code.** Fiedler 2010, Floating Point Determinism (https://gafferongames.com/post/floating_point_determinism/). Implication: floats under a pinned mode ship; the risk named is exactly a library entering the simulation.
17. **League of Legends stayed on floats; its one-in-5,000-games divergence was an uninitialised vector, found by bisecting 580,000 instances.** Hoskinson 2018, Riot Games (https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/determinism-league-legends-fixing-divergences). Implication: hash every field the step reads, so an uninitialised value cannot hide.
18. **Factorio feared cross-machine float differences and found them not to be a problem; it wrote its own trig, and its real desync was `std::sort` ordering equal elements differently across compilers.** Wube 2014, FFF-52 (https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-52). Implication: every ordering in the step is by explicit key; no library sort touches state.
19. **Photon Quantum replaces every float with a Q48.16 fixed-point type in one 64-bit integer, uses lookup tables for trig and square root, and pays a step of about 1.5e-5 per unit; converting from float inside the simulation "will cause desyncs 100% of the time."** Exit Games, Quantum 3 manual (https://doc.photonengine.com/quantum/current/manual/quantum-ecs/fixed-point). Implication: fixed point is the answer for arbitrary user code on unpinned runtimes; it is not free, and it does not remove the transcendental problem.
20. **The Rust `fixed` crate provides no sin, cos, exp, or pow because implementations trade off differently; fixed point still requires pinning those functions yourself.** https://docs.rs/fixed/latest/fixed/. Implication: the arithmetic decision is about pinning, not about the number format.
21. **Unity's deterministic float mode shipped only in Burst 1.8.25 (2025), flushes subnormals, is 64-bit only, and does not promise identical NaN bits.** Unity Burst 1.8 manual and Unity staff forum post (https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.burst@1.8/manual/compilation-burstcompile.html; https://discussions.unity.com/t/what-is-the-current-state-of-the-cross-platform-determinism-of-unity-physics/798011). Implication: subnormal handling is a pin the WASM path must state.

### QD Solver state and shapes

22. **Jolt's `SaveState` writes per body the velocities, forces, sleep-test spheres, the sleep timer and allow-sleeping flag, and the contact cache sorted by body pair with normals, friction impulses, and per-point non-penetration impulses; islands are rebuilt from scratch every step; broadphase query order and listener order are not deterministic; the sleep rule accumulates elapsed time against 0.5 s.** Jolt `StateRecorder.h`, `MotionProperties.cpp`, `ContactConstraintManager.cpp`, Architecture doc, opened 2026-09-24. Implication: warm-start impulses and sleep timers are state and are hashed in canonical order; islands and broadphase order are recomputed and never feed state; sleep is by quanta count in our law, never by seconds.
23. **Rapier's full snapshot includes the island manager, broadphase, narrowphase, body, collider and joint sets, the CCD solver, integration parameters and gravity, and claims identical bytes across machines after the same steps; each contact stores warm-start, tangent, and twist impulses, and warm-starting from a rotated basis "kicks resting stacks".** Rapier serialization and determinism guides and `ContactData` source, opened 2026-09-24 (https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/serialization). Implication: if a library is adopted, its whole snapshot is the hashed state, not a subset.
24. **Heightfields must be static in Jolt; meshes may move only if they never touch another mesh or heightfield; active edges are marked at construction; internal-edge removal covers shared edges within one shape and not T-junctions or edges between bodies; Rapier discourages trimeshes on dynamic bodies and recommends `FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES`; Box2D chain shapes make collision one-sided to kill ghost collisions.** Jolt Architecture doc; Rapier colliders guide (https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/colliders); Catto 2020 (https://box2d.org/posts/2020/06/ghost-collisions/). Implication: terrain is a static heightfield, props are static meshes with edge flags or convex hulls, dynamic bodies are convex only, and mesh-against-mesh is refused at load.
25. **Warm starting carries clamped accumulated impulses across steps; Box2D matches contact points across steps by feature ids to drive it; a solver that stacks without velocity warm start still carries friction anchors, which is state.** Catto 2024, Solver2D (https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/); Box2D simulation docs. Implication: there is no stacking solver without cross-step state; the law hashes it or refuses stacking.
